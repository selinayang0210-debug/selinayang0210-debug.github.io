About

Hi, I’m Selina.

I tend to linger on questions I haven’t quite figured out.

A flow might feel seamless, an answer might sound convincing, and I’ll still wonder: Why does it work this way? What does this choice change? Would the logic hold if we changed an assumption?

I like unpacking those questions, then making something to see whether my understanding holds up. Over time, that habit has shaped the kind of design I want to pursue.

My creative practice began with acting.

I studied acting at Beijing Film Academy and the University of Plymouth. After graduating, I took time to explore different creative tools and mediums independently.

My explorations ranged from installation art to UX and service design, with a particular interest in people with disabilities and different cognitive needs. I became curious about different ways of perceiving, expressing, and interacting—and how creative work could respond to those experiences. I also discovered the satisfaction of turning an unclear problem into something tangible.

I brought those explorations to Vancouver Film School, where I studied Digital Design and expanded what I could make.

From exploring independently to creating together.

Today, I’m pursuing a Master of Digital Media at the Centre for Digital Media, working with multidisciplinary teams on client projects through research, interaction design, and prototyping.

In a team, someone’s question can change an idea. Making something together can take it somewhere none of us expected. These experiences have made me more interested in how people build shared understanding, make decisions, and turn conversations into action.

Now, I bring these questions into AI product design.

When AI can produce so many answers so quickly, I wonder: Does a person’s emerging idea become clearer through the process, or get swept along by an answer that already feels complete?

I’m interested in the space between an AI suggestion and a human decision: how people explore possibilities, recognize their intentions, understand changes, and choose what comes next.

That interest connects Mize and Orbit. Across individual creativity and team collaboration, I’m exploring how suggestions become decisions. Before committing, people need to understand what they’re choosing. Afterwards, they should be able to see what that decision changed.

I’m still discovering how I want to work with AI myself. I use it to explore, organize, and prototype, and I enjoy the unexpected ideas it brings. I also want to examine its suggestions, question its assumptions, and make changes before forming my own judgment.

Those are the tools I want to keep designing: tools that give people room to shape an idea—and discover something they hadn’t imagined yet.
